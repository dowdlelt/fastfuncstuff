### Takeaway

This establishes correctness of the supported forward equations, not physiological identifiability from arbitrary estimated curves.

## Which model details materially affect the interpretation of response curves?

### Takeaway
Keep the reference numerical conventions explicit, and distinguish positive modulation B from the baseline self-inhibition sigma. Neither paper labels nor printed prior values can safely substitute for the actual transformed equations.

### Cited Findings
- The FFS baseline matrix is `A=-sigma*exp(diag(A))` on its diagonal, and condition modulation adds `u*B`. Thus **increasing baseline sigma increases self-inhibition**, whereas **positive diagonal B reduces self-inhibition** during modulation. The model's neuronal equation also subtracts `mu*xI`, with `dxI=lam*(xE-xI)`. B is a change in a dynamical connection with Hz units, not a percent BOLD difference or a direct neuronal amplitude. [FFS equations](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/fastfuncstuff/laminar/forward.py:239), [Faes equation implementation](https://github.com/lonikefaes/predictive_tones/blob/main/Code_laminar_BOLD_model/LBR_model_fx.m).
- Faes's paper Table 2 prints mu=-0.8 and lambda=1.8. In the driver these are **log deviations**: `pE.mu=-0.8`, `pE.lam=1.8`, multiplied through prior anchors `P0.mu=1.5`, `P0.lam=0.1`. Actual physical means are approximately **mu=0.674 Hz and lambda=0.605 Hz**. FFS preserves these parameterizations. Similarly s_d=0 in the fitted log parameter means baseline slope 0.4, not no AV slope. [Faes driver](https://github.com/lonikefaes/predictive_tones/blob/main/apply_laminar_BOLD_model.m), [FFS priors](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/fastfuncstuff/laminar/experiment.py:79).
- Faes fixed adaptation parameters and restricted modulation to the self-connection because the short ITI did not sufficiently observe the undershoot to identify inhibitory-connection modulation. Whether that restriction is adequate for another experiment depends on its measured response and timing, not simply code parity. [Faes methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73540-z).
- The code deliberately uses `ep_v` for the AV intravascular `k2d` term to match the reference. The compartment-specific paper expression would use `ep_d`. This is already documented and explicitly preserved; no silent change was made. [FFS observation constants](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/fastfuncstuff/laminar/forward.py:302), [original MATLAB implementation](https://github.com/martinhavlicek/laminar_BOLD_model/blob/master/LBR_model.m).
- `jacobian="reference"` deliberately reproduces the generated MATLAB derivative with respect to exponentiated log-state variables; `jacobian="exact"` uses the derivative with respect to the actual stored state. The reference mode's Taylor correction is `J D^-1 f`, whereas exact is `J f`. The existing tests establish that these differ and that the reference mode matches the stored MATLAB result. [FFS integration](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/fastfuncstuff/laminar/integrate.py), [reference symbolic generator](https://github.com/lonikefaes/predictive_tones/blob/main/Code_laminar_BOLD_model/LBR_gen_fx_fcn.m).
- The Faes branch assumes equal inflation/deflation viscoelastic constants; FFS raises for asymmetric tau flags. The full 2020 physiological family includes asymmetric inflation/deflation, which this implementation deliberately does not support. [FFS cache validation](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/fastfuncstuff/laminar/forward.py:225), [original vascular code](https://github.com/martinhavlicek/laminar_BOLD_model/blob/master/LBR_model.m).
- Current BOLD constants are anchored to 7 T GE: tissue and blood R2* and r0 are fixed numbers. Merely changing B0 scales susceptibility terms but does not recompute these field-dependent quantities; similarly converting to SE requires a justified signal model and constants. TE is seconds. [FFS fixed constants](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/fastfuncstuff/laminar/params.py:83), [2020 Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116209), [2018 physiology review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.063).

### Inferences
- For applications outside the published auditory paradigm, compare fixed/free adaptation and vascular assumptions as sensitivity analyses. A poor curve fit or posterior at extreme sigma should trigger investigation, not an interpretation that the model has directly measured neural excitability.
- Keep parity mode for replication. An exact-Jacobian scientific validation should use a small-step convergence reference rather than treating agreement with a known reference quirk as mathematical correctness.

### Gaps
- No fresh convergence measurement comparing reference/exact integration against a high-accuracy ODE solver was completed in this task.
- The small effect size of the ep_v/ep_d discrepancy quoted in the earlier wiki review was not remeasured here.

## What does the physiology support, and what remains unidentifiable?

### Takeaway
The model is appropriate for condition-dependent neuronal hypotheses under constrained physiology, but neither draining correction nor an excellent forward fit establishes arbitrary laminar neuronal truth.

### Cited Findings
- The 2021 paper recommends seven to ten BOLD depths for three neuronal depths; the neuronal units are deliberately independent because adding unobserved interlayer coupling can undermine distinguishability. Condition differences are generally more faithful than absolute single-condition depth profiles. These are model/measurement constraints, not a requirement for sub-TR temporal knots. [2021 paper, sections 3.4-3.6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2021.102055).
- The 2021 sensitivity simulation finds AV baseline-CBV slopes below truth can give qualitatively wrong neuronal profiles; fitting the slope, or fixing it at/slightly above truth, performed better in that example. The authors recommend sensitivity analyses on crucial physiological parameters. [2021 paper, Fig. 10 and section 3.6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2021.102055).
- The 2021 discussion explicitly leaves remote pial-vein blooming unresolved: a draining-vein model does not automatically remove magnetic-field effects extending into neighboring voxels. ROI curvature, B0 orientation, finite voxel sampling, and assumed PSF are additional observation effects. [2021 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2021.102055), [2018 review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.063).

### Inferences
- Reliable TR-spaced TENT curves and a forward model projected through the same estimator are compatible with this physiology. There is no physiological justification here for forcing the user to finer-than-TR knots; temporal identifiability and the estimator's transfer operator must be handled in the observation layer.
- Parent's timing/inference reviewers should distinguish a coefficient-space observation model from a direct time-sample comparison, and identify covariance consequences. This physiological audit does not validate the latter shortcut.

### Follow-up: independent integration accuracy check (completed)

The previously open convergence gap above is now closed for one representative trajectory. `scripts/laminar_curve_review/integration_audit.py` compares the production `integrate` routine (eager CPU float64) with **SciPy DOP853 rtol=1e-11, atol=1e-13** on the same continuous RHS. The reference solver is split at every input discontinuity. N=3, K=7, Faes physiological baselines and adaptation log-means, unit drive at all neuronal depths plus deep B=0.6 on [1,2) s, outputs t=0,0.5,...11.5 s. Input duration and observation times are identical at both step sizes: no SPM end-inclusive extra bin and no changing microtime/sample delay. These controls isolate integration error. [Audit script](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/scripts/laminar_curve_review/integration_audit.py), [complete results](/home/logan/Dropbox/Resources/code/fastfuncstuff/research_notes/Laminar%20timing%20and%20model%20validation/integration_results.json).

| reference | 0.05 | 0.001586 | 0.000706 | 0.0760% |

| reference | 0.025 | 0.000896 | 0.000404 | 0.0429% |
| exact | 0.025 | 0.000254 | 0.000073 | 0.0122% |

DOP853 peak was 2.0871% signal change. Halving dt reduced RMS error **1.75x for reference** and **4.03x for exact**. The latter is consistent with the expected second-order convergence of the deterministic Taylor method; the former reveals the first-order error introduced by the missing chain factor. Reference/exact outputs differed by at most **0.002033 percentage points** at dt=0.05, or **0.0974% of the reference-solver peak**, and 0.001015 percentage points at dt=0.025. Peak-amplitude biases across the seven depths at dt=0.05 were +0.017% to +0.070% (reference) and -0.004% to -0.016% (exact).

**Interpretation:** the known Jacobian quirk is mathematically real and reduces convergence order, but its prediction difference is small in this representative stable response. It does not explain the much larger coarse-TENT curve distortions already under review. This one trajectory does not bound effects at extreme posterior parameters, with prolonged/repeated inputs, or on model free energy/rankings. No inversion was rerun using these modes, so numerical proximity is not evidence that scientific inferences are always unchanged. The RHS was shared with FFS deliberately: this is an independent **integrator** check, supplementing rather than replacing the physical-conservation and MATLAB-equation checks.
